Weighted R² per Predictor

barangay share of each typhoon's packs · leave-one-typhoon-out

R² is the share of the variation in how relief is split between barangays that a predictor explains: 1 = perfect, 0 = no better than giving every barangay the average share, below 0 = worse. Weighted: every barangay-typhoon row counts in proportion to that typhoon's total packs, so large relief operations matter more than storms where only a few packs were given. Each typhoon is predicted only from the other typhoons, so these are out-of-sample scores. Higher is better.

Stock sufficiency (P90)

How to read these numbers

Each figure compares the model against real typhoons it did not see when making that prediction (leave-one-typhoon-out validation). Share MAE is how far off the predicted split across barangays is, on average, from what actually happened - lower is better. It's compared against two simple baselines: splitting relief equally across every barangay, and just using each barangay's average historical share.

Honest reading: the model beats the equal-split baseline in every municipality, but in Calasiao - where there's the most real history per barangay - simply averaging each barangay's past share does about as well. That's expected: with enough history, a plain average becomes a strong predictor on its own. The model's real advantage is giving a sensible number to a barangay with little or no history of its own, which a plain average can't do. Re-check as more real records are loaded.
