how the {{ forecast_months }}-month forecast is shared among barangays · Hide
  1. The model forecasts the whole municipality ({{ "{:,}".format(lgu_forecast.horizon_total) }} packs expected, {{ "{:,}".format(lgu_forecast.horizon_p90) }} recommended stockpile) from real per-typhoon relief records - a single barangay has too few typhoons of its own to forecast independently.
  2. Each barangay gets a share of that total from a regression fit on every real relief event this municipality has on record: share = family count × a fitted rate, clipped so it can't go negative and rescaled so every barangay's share adds up to 100%.
  3. Barangay figure = share × municipal figure. Shares add up to 100%, so barangay numbers always add up to the municipality.

Per-municipality validation

The tiles above show only the worst-performing LGU's MAE. Here's every LGU, so a strong or weak result in one municipality isn't hidden inside a single combined number. Plain MAE counts every typhoon equally, so storms where only a few packs went to one or two barangays (which no method can predict) weigh as much as major relief operations; size-weighted MAE weights each typhoon by its total packs, showing how much relief actually lands in the wrong barangay. "Pooled history" - each barangay's share of all past relief - is the strongest simple baseline. Because there are only 8-14 typhoons per municipality, the comparison with it is given as the number of held-out typhoons where the model did better, and a 95% interval for the difference in size-weighted MAE (negative means the model is better; an interval that crosses 0 means no clear difference).

How to read these numbers

Each figure compares the model against real typhoons it did not see when making that prediction (leave-one-typhoon-out validation). Share MAE is how far off the predicted split across barangays is, on average, from what actually happened - lower is better. It's compared against two simple baselines: splitting relief equally across every barangay, and just using each barangay's average historical share.

Honest reading: the model beats the equal-split baseline in every municipality, but in Calasiao - where there's the most real history per barangay - simply averaging each barangay's past share does about as well. That's expected: with enough history, a plain average becomes a strong predictor on its own. The model's real advantage is giving a sensible number to a barangay with little or no history of its own, which a plain average can't do. Re-check as more real records are loaded.
